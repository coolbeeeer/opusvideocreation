# The Bacteria That Moved Into Your Cells: script and accuracy notes

Vertical Short / Reel, 50.6 s, 1080x1920, 30 fps. 125 words (about 150 words per minute), 8 lines.
Colour code: orange is the mitochondria and their bacterial ancestor and nothing else, so the viewer links the two;
the host cell is lavender, muscle tissue crimson.

## Accuracy notes
Each line, what it claims, and what the picture simplifies. Wording was checked against the sources below.

1. **"Powered by bacteria. Well, former bacteria."** Mitochondria descend from an alphaproteobacterium that became
   an endosymbiont ([PMC3428767](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3428767/)). Muscles run on ATP; in steady
   activity like walking most of it is made by mitochondria. Short bursts also use other ATP sources (glycolysis,
   creatine phosphate), so "powered by" is meant in the everyday sense.
2. **"Thousands of mitochondria" in a muscle cell.** The NIH describes muscle cells as having hundreds or thousands
   of mitochondria ([NIH Research Matters](https://www.nih.gov/news-events/nih-research-matters/mitochondria-health)).
   Not every cell has them: mature red blood cells have none.
3. **"Roughly two billion years ago."** Estimates for the origin of mitochondria range from about 1.5 to 2 billion
   years ago ([PubMed 42412246](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42412246/),
   [Science Advances](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.adj4493)); a molecular-clock study puts the
   alphaproteobacterial ancestors at about 1.9 billion years
   ([Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-23645-4)). "Roughly two billion" is the older
   end of that range, which is why both tags say "~2 billion".
4. **"A bigger cell swallowed a bacterium ... It moved in."** This is the classic picture. The host was most likely
   an archaeon (related to today's Asgard archaea), not a cell like ours, and whether the partner was swallowed
   (engulfed) or the two grew together more gradually is still debated. The host is drawn as a generic blob.
5. **"The guest got shelter and food. The host got energy."** A simplification of the deal. Early on the partners
   probably traded other metabolites (hydrogen-based models are one proposal); exporting ATP to the host came later.
6. **"Their own DNA, a double wall, split in two, just like bacteria."** Mitochondria keep a small circular genome,
   have two membranes and multiply by splitting, like bacteria
   ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/science/What-evidence-supports-the-idea-that-mitochondria-evolved-from-ancient-bacteria);
   division: [PMC6424925](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6424925/)). They can no longer live on their
   own: most of their genes moved to the cell's nucleus, and their division uses machinery the host provides.
7. **"They turn food and oxygen into ATP, your cells' fuel."** Food is first broken down outside the mitochondria;
   the mitochondria burn the products with oxygen and make most of the cell's ATP. ATP is the energy carrier the cell
   spends ([NIGMS](https://nigms.nih.gov/biobeat/2022/11/science-snippet-atps-amazing-power)).
8. **"About your own body weight of it every single day."** The body holds little ATP at any moment and recycles it
   constantly, turning over roughly its own weight per day
   ([NIGMS](https://nigms.nih.gov/biobeat/2022/11/science-snippet-atps-amazing-power)). The balance shows a day's
   turnover, not a stock.
9. **"Every one of yours came from your mother."** Mitochondria are inherited through the egg
   ([PubMed 11041517](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11041517/)); the sperm's few mitochondria are destroyed after
   fertilisation, largely by autophagy ([PMC3518432](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3518432/)). Rare cases
   of fathers' mitochondrial DNA being passed on have been reported and are debated
   ([PMC6358698](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6358698/)). "Every one" is true for practically everyone.

Picture simplifications:
- The dive compresses scale. In the picture the thigh is about 1,200 mitochondria wide (the last frame is zoomed about
  8,300x on the wide shot); a real thigh, about 15 cm, would be 150,000 or more (a mitochondrion is 0.5-1 µm wide).
- Mitochondria are drawn as orange capsules with eyes, lined up between the myofibrils. Real ones also form branching
  networks.
- The enzymes biting and bouncing off is a cartoon of digestion failing; coins stand for ATP molecules.
- The walker's leg lights in the finale are a visual metaphor, not glowing tissue.
